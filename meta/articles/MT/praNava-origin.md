> It appears in quite archaic layers of the Sāmaveda-Saṁhitā as a stobha, a non-lexical poetic refrain of sorts.

When considering the praNava, its most fundamental and primary deployment should not be forgotten: in the recitation of the RV in the shrauta ritual the OMkAra is inserted into the R^ik-s -- the shastra recitation. 

The shastra-s begin with the incantation OM shoM; shoMsAvOM etc. At the end of special chants, like the sAmidhenI-s, which have the usual inserted 3 mAtra OMkAra-s, there is 4 mAtra OM. 

All these shrauta uses are laid out clearly in the taittirIya shruti:

> om iti brahma | om itIdagM sarvam |  
om ity etad anukR^itir hasma vA apy oshrAvayetyAshrAvayanti |  
om iti sAmAni gAyanti |   
ogM shom iti shastrANi shagMsanti |   
om ity adhvaryuH pratigaraM pratigR^iNAti |  
om iti brahmA prasauti |  
om ity agnihotram anujAnAti | 
om iti brAhmaNaH pravakShyann Aha brahmopApnavAnIti | brahmaivopApnoti  ||
