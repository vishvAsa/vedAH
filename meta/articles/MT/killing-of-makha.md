title = "Killing of makha"
+++

Source: [TW](https://x.com/blog_supplement/status/2092858600775459003)

There are several occurrences of the killing of "makha" throughout the shruti. What exactly was meant by this makha still remains a bit mysterious. In the R^igveda we encounter our ancient ancestors smiting makha:

> pra sunvAnasya+andhaso   
marto na vR^ita tad vachaH |  
apa shvAnam arAdhasaM   
hatA makhaM na bhR^igavaH ||  
RV 9.101.13   

> Like a mortal, he chose for himself that speech  
Of the pressed [soma] stalk  
Drive away the illiberal dog! 
smite [him], like the bhR^igus [did to] makha!

Again, in the RV, we hear of indra bearing away the head of makha in the sUkta of iTant bhArgava:

> tvam makhasya dodhataH   
shiro .ava tvacho bharaH | 
agachChaH somino gR^iham || RV 10.171.2

> You, the impetuous makha's
head bore away from his skin.  
[You] went to the house of the soma-sacrificer.

The Yajurveda mentions multiple gods slaying makha, e.g., in TS 3.2.4:

> namo .agnaye makhaghne makhasya mA yasho .aryAt |  
namo rudrAya makhaghne namaskR^ityA mA pAhi |  
nama indrAya makhaghna  
indriyam me vIryam mA nir vadhIH |  

> Homage to agni, the slayer of makha! May the glory of makha reach me!  
Homage to rudra, the slayer of makha! Reverencing you, protect me!  
Homage to indra, the slayer of makha!   
Strike not away my sensory power nor my vigor!

In the ritual, the severed head of the makha is identified with the ukhA pot (in TS 5.1.6):

> makhasya shiro .asi+ity Aha  
yaj~no vai makhaH tasyaitach Chiro yad ukhA |

The pa~nchaviMsha brAhmaNa of the kauthuma sAmaveda gives us a clue for the severing of the head of the makha by equating it with the pravargya pot (in PB 7.5):

> devA vai yashas-kAmAH sattram Asata+  
agnir indro vAyur  
makhas te .abruvann  
yan no yasha R^ichChAt  
tan naH sahAsad iti |  
teShA makhaM yasha ArchChat |  
tad AdAyApAkrAmat  
tadasya prAsahAditsanta  
taM paryayatanta svadhanuH pratiShThAbhyAtiShThat |  
tasya dhanur Artnir UrdhvA patitvA shiro .achChinat  
sa pravargyo .abhavad |  
yaj~no vai makho  
yat pravargyaM pravR^i~njanti  
yaj~nasyaiva tach ChiraH pratidadhati ||  

> The gods, desirous of glory, performed a sacrificial session: agni, indra, vAyu, and makha. They said: 'Whichever of us glory shall reach, that shall belong to us all in common.'  Glory reached makha among them. Taking it, he departed. They wished to take it from him by force. They surrounded him. He stood, resting his chin upon the end of his strung bow. The tip of his bow string sprang upward and severed his head. It (the head) became the pravargya. makha, truly, is the sacrifice. When they perform the pravargya, they restore that very head of the sacrifice.

This brAhmaNa maps onto the slaying of the makha by the three gods agni, rudra and indra in the TS via the vAyu-rudra congruence, which is also expressed elsewhere in the sAmavedic tradition.

We'll explain the idiosyncratic philosophical significance of this to us elsewhere.
