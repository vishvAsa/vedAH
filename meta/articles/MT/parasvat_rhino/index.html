The parasvant: the rhino, the ass and the Germans

Throughout all layers of the shruti, an animal called the parasvant is mentioned.

In the R^igveda and the atharvaveda it is explicitly associated with sexual potency and appears to have been eaten for that purpose.

RV

In the RV, after the two explicit R^ik-s by indra and indrANI arguing for the supremacy of the genitalia of their respective sexes, indrANI states that vR^iShAkapi has found a slain parasvant along with the implements to cut it up and cook it.

AV

In both AV saMhitA-s, an incantation is provided for the male intromitant organ of the ritual patron to develop like that of the parasvant by means of the even more mysterious medicinal product termed the tayAdara:

athA pasas tAyAdaraM
vAtena sthUlabhaM kR^itam | yAvat parasvataH pasas
tAvat te vardhatAM pasaH ||
AV-vulgate 6.72.2

The AV-P provides two further incantations mentioning this animal:

viSham ugraM viShaM sthiraM
viShasya mahimA mahAn |
viShaM parasvataH shiraH
shatadhA vy apAtayat ||
AV-P 19.47.10

The poison is fierce, the poison is stubborn,
great is the power of the poison!
[Yet] the head of the parasvant has
shattered the poison into a hundred pieces.

The 2nd incantation is precisely the type that, in the old days, White indologists would render in Latin. We too are not translating it in full so as not to trivialize a medical scripture in a vulgar-sounding modern rendering:

yathA parasvAM Chardhate
gardabhaH kR^idhu-sandR^ishaH |
evA te shardhatAM bhasad
dhAlIkShNaparNa shardhaya
vR^itrahA shAkI shardhaya || AV-P 19.51.11

It effectively invokes indra to relieve blocked digestive wind via the carminative hAlIkShNaparNa (lynx-leaf) plant in the manner of the digestive release of a parasvant and a donkey.

YV

In the yajurveda saMhitA-s, the parasvant is mentioned as one of the sacrificial wild animals in ashvamedha ritual. The taittirIya and kaTha samhitA-s state that the parasvant is offered to kAma (kAmAya parasvAn |);
however, the maitrAyaNIya and shuklayajurveda saMhitA-s state that the parasvant is offered to IshAna (i.e., rudra; IshAnAya parasvata Alabhate |).

The association with kAma is particularly apt given the sexual association of the parasvant in the AV and the RV saMhitA-s.

The alternative association with rudra might also connect the fertility of the deity and the animal. In his commentary on the TS, sAyaNa glosses parasvant as a type of buffalo.+++(4)+++

jaiminIya brAhmaNa

An association (not synonymy) of the parasvant with buffalos or wild bulls is explicitly presented in a colorful metaphor for the pR^iShTha songs and their arrangement in the laud in the jaiminIya brAhmana:

atho yathA pitA mAtaivaM bR^ihad-rathantare, yathA putrA evaM pR^iShThAni | yad vai putro .atipAdayati pitA vai tasya shamayitA pitA niSheddhA | tad yad bR^ihad-rathantare abhito bhavatash shAntyA eva niShiddhyai | atho yathA pitarau putrAn abhita parishayyAtAM tAdR^ik tad yad bR^ihad-rathantare abhito bhavato, madhye pR^iShThAni | yathA ha vA idam R^iShabhA vA parasvanto vA samutkruddhAs tiShTheyur evam etad yad etAni madhyataH pR^iShThAni bhavanti, tad yach Chyaita-naudhase abhito bhavataH |

Now, as the father and mother [are to each other], so are the bR^ihat and rathantara; as sons, so are the pR^iShTha-s (evidently here they are the following sAman-s: vairUpa, vairAja, shAkvara, and raivata). For when a son transgresses, the father is indeed his pacifier, the father is his restrainer. That is why the bR^ihat and rathantara are on both sides: precisely for pacification, for restraint.

Moreover, just as two parents would lie down encircling their sons on both sides, likewise so it is that the bR^ihat and rathantara are on both sides, with the pR^iShTha-s in the middle.

Just as wild bulls or parasvant-s might stand up here in great agitation, so it is with these pR^iShTha-s in the middle. That is why the shyaita-naudhasa [sAman-s] are on both sides.

Here, the pR^iShTha-sAman-s are said to be “wild” like the wild bulls or the parasvant-s; hence, they need to be corralled by the bR^ihad-rathantara and the shyaita-naudhasa. This clinches the parasvant being a wild beast.

kauShitaki-brAhmaNa-upaniShad

In the kauShitaki-brAhmaNa-upaniShad, the parasvant (or a variant there of) comes in a long list of animals one many be born as:

sa iha kITo vA pata~Ngo vA shakunir vA shArdUlo vA siMho vA matsyo vA paras(h)vA vA puruSho vA .anyo vaiteShu sthAneShu pratyAjAyat |

He is reborn here in these bodies: either as a worm, or an insect, or a bird, or a tiger, or a lion, or a fish, or a parasvan, or a human, or as some other [being].

Here, the commentators gloss parasvan as a snake.

Finally, the baudhAyana-shrauta-sUtra has an incantation to station unpleasant elements in oneself elsewhere: parasvati me .asamR^iddhiH khaDge ma Artih | In the parasvant my failure, in the rhinoceros my misfortune [is placed].

prAkRta

Beyond the Vedic texts, a cognate word is found in the vulgar pALi (sudhAbhojana jAtaka = 535, and vidhurapaNDita jAtaka = 545) and the prAkR^ita of the ashokan inscriptions. In all these instances, it occurs in a long list of animals comparable to the kauShitaki-BU and is rendered as palAsAda and palasata, respectively. For example, the long list of animals that ashoka proscribes from harming goes thus:

devAnAmpiye piyadasi lAja hevam ahA saDuvIsati-vasa-abhisitena me imAni jAtAni avadhiyAni kaTAni seyathA suke sAlikA alune chakacAke haMse nandImukhe gelATe jatUkA ambA-kapIlikA daLI anaThika-maChe vedaveyake gaMgA-pupuTake saMkuja-maChe kaphaTa-sayake paMna-sase simale saMDake okapiMDe palasate seta-kapote gAma-kapote save chatupade ye paTibhogaM no eti na cha khAdiyatI <> eLakA chA sUkalI chA gabhinI va pAyamInA va avadhiya…

After the mauryan age, this mysterious animal parasvant falls out of the contemporary Indian vocabulary.

German attempts

What exactly was the parasvant? The Indian commentators seem to be mostly in the dark in this regard – they recognize it as a wild animal – beyond that, their conjectures are not supported by any evidence. With the emergence of modern indology, a whole succession of German researchers have attempted to solve the problem.

Wild ass?

First, Rudolf von Roth suggested that the parasvant was a wild ass. This proposal is consistent with its fertility role, given the well-known size of the male intromitent organ of the said animal. This association continues to this date in modern Chinese medicine, whose insatiable appetite for the said equid is endangering the donkey in Africa.

Georg Bühler

Contra von Roth, Georg Bühler then noticed the cognacy with the pALi word and suggested that it meant ‘rhinoceros’. This proposal has been accepted and solidified by two further German indologists, Lüders and Bautze.

Harry Falk

More recently, another German indologist, Harry Falk, has claimed that, whereas the well-known word for the rhinoceros, khaDga, stood for the Greater one-horned rhino, parasvant meant the smaller one-horned Javan rhino. He believes that these two were differentially depicted even on the Harappan seals.

He then takes quite a flight of fancy, claiming that the Copper Hoard/Ocher Colored Pottery culture produced their famous harpoons to disembowel the parasvant by stabbing it in the arse. However, there is precious little in the early or middle Indo-Aryan literature to identify the animal definitively.

Evaluation

Below we evaluate the support for two competing hypotheses:

The parasvant as a wild ass:

  1. The intromitant male organ of the wild ass is consistent with the first of the AV incantations.
  2. In the wind-relieving incantation of the AV-P, the parasvant is mentioned along with a gardabha, the domestic donkey; hence, by association, it could be a version of it.
  3. In the spells of the kuntApa sUkta-s AV-vul 20,131.18-19 could be taken together: adUhamityAM pUShakam ||18|| atyardharcha parasvataH ||19|| They have milked the mulberry tree (colostrum?): one-and-a half of the parasvant. If this holds, then we have a parasvant being milked – it is more likely to be an ass than a rhino.

The parasvant as rhino:

  1. The intromitant male organ of the rhino and its long copulation are consistent with a sexual role associated with the parasvant across the saMhitA-s.
  2. In the baudhAyana-shrauta-sUtra, it is mentioned alongside the khaDga, a well-known and confirmed name for the rhino. Hence, by association, it could be a type of rhino.
  3. If the pALi and prAkR^ita cognate words are understood as rhino, then it might be retention of an older memory in Middle Indo-Aryan. However, it should be noted that there is no clear account of the palasata in any MIA text to unambiguously call it a rhino.
  4. The poison-quelling incantation of the AV-P mentions the poison as shattered by the head of the parasvant – this would be more consistent with the horn of a rhino destroying poison.

In conclusion, we still find the evidence equivocal. We consider it an important problem because it being a rhino or an ass would have profound implications for the biogeography of the sUkta-s that mention it.

Not nIlgAy

There are other Sanskrit words that seem more likely for nIlgAy: R^ishya, gokarNa and gomR^iga. Its trachaea is used for offering blood of the horse in the ashvamedha.
