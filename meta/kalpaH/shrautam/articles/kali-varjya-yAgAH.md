The Aśvamedhá, Gosavá, and Rājasū́ya are no longer practiced as they are kalivárjya.
It is not out of deliberate erasure.

However, several features of these rituals are simply impractical today.

For example, the Rājasū́ya cannot be performed in the absence of a committee of ratnín-s appointed by the monarch  
and the Aśvamedhá should be organized by a sovereign monarch, preferably (albeit not necessarily) a sārvabhaumá ruler (rā́jā sārvabhaumó 'śvamedhéna yajeta, ápy ásārvabhaumaḥ - Āpastamba-Śrauta-Sūtra XX.1.1).

## Imprecation
Later texts like Harivaṃśa justify the prohibition through a Brāhmaṇá's imprecation against the ruler Janamejayá Pārikṣitá.

The Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa alleges that Śatā́nīka, son of Sattrājít, conducted the Góvinata variant of the Aśvamedhá sacrifice after seizing the horse of the king of the Kāśī́-s (Góvinatena Śatā́nīka Sātrājitá īje, Kā́śyasyā́śvam ādā́ya)

## Performance
The Śuṅga ruler Puṣyamitra is famed for his "revival" of the Aśvamedha and the last attested Aśvamedha was actually surprisingly recent. It was organized by the Kachvāhā ruler Jai Singh II in the 18th century.

## utsannatA
One could argue that not all particularities regarding the sacrifice were properly understood even in Vedic times.

The Taittirīya-Saṃhitā refers to it as a decayed sacrifice:

> uthsannayajñó vā́ eṣá yád Aśvamedháḥ kás tád vedéty āhur yádi sárvo vā kriyáte ná vā sárva íti  
They say "The Aśvamedhá is a decayed sacrifice. Who knows whether everything is really performed or everything isn't performed?"

A similar statement is found in the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa, which interestingly contains the most comprehensive and systematic description of the Aśvamedha:

> utsannayajñá iva vā́ eṣá yád Aśvamedháḥ kíṃ vā hy etásya kriyáte kíṃ vā ná  
The Aśvamedhá is, as it were, a decayed sacrifice. What part of it is performed and what is not?
