An assessment of muṇḍaka Upanishad:

Guru Parampara

The self attested guruparamparā is brahmā the first born of the devas –> atharvan, his eldest son –> āngiras –> satyavāha bhāradvāja–> various others –> an āngiras –> śaunaka mahāśāla.

This is not exactly the creation order in gopatha brahmaṇa.
It has svambhuva brahman –> bhṛgu–> atharvan, who is Prajapati –> angiras –> 20 angiras ṛṣis responsible for 20 kāṇḍas of ātharvaṇa shruti.

From the testimony of brahmaṇas from other vedas, this most likely is a bhṛgu reworking of an atharvan cosmogony and the muṇḍaka lineage has a genuine atharvanic stamp.

Ritual rules extractable from muṇḍaka:

1. The mukhāhuti (muṇḍaka 1.2.2):

yadā lelāyate hy arciḥ samiddhe havyavāhane | tadājyabhāgāv antareṇāhutīḥ pratipādayed śraddhayā hutam śraddhayā hutam om | In a yajña, the ājyabhāgas are considered the eyes. Offering the ājyabhāgas symbolically opens the yajña’s eyes. The spot for the main oblations, the “mouth” is in between the eyes towards the center. muṇḍaka’s rule “well fueled and blazing agni, the mukhāhuti “offered with faith x2 OM” in between the ājyabhāgas” is at home in any number of śrauta or even gṛhya texts.

2. Obligatory sacrifices (muṇḍaka 1.2.3)

yasyāgnihotram adarśam apaurṇamāsam acāturmāsyam anāgrayaṇam atithivarjitaṃ ca| ahutam avaiśvadevam avidhinā hutam ā saptamāṃs tasya lokān hinasti || The agnihotra of someone destroys the 7 worlds of someone who doesn’t offer darśapūrṇamāsa fortnightly sacrifices, cāturmāsya seasonal sacrifices, āgrayaṇa where fresh harvested grains are offered (it is so important that it is obligatory even for someone who hasn’t setup the 3 fires and it is to be offered in gṛhya fire), manuṣya yajña (food offered to guests), and vaiśvadeva. Same result if it is offered untimely or with violated injunctions. This more or less tallies with the obligatory śrauta and other sacrifices we can infer from brahmaṇas and sUtras of other vedas.

3. The Ahutis to tongues of agni

In paippalāda agnimukham, Ahutis are given to the 7 tongues of agni - kālī, karālī, manojavā, sulohitā, sudhūmravarṇā, suliṅginī and viśvarucī. muṇḍaka more or less has the same names: (muṇḍaka 1.2.4) kālī karālī ca manojavā ca sulohitā yā ca sudhūmravarṇā | sphuliṅginī viśvarucī ca devī lelāyamānā iti sapta jihvāḥ || The name sphuliṅginī of muṇḍaka appears to be the pAThabheda of suliṅginī. The jihvāhutis is homologous to the agni archana/alaṅkaraṇa step in yajurvedic agnimukham to the 8 forms of agni - agni, jātavedas, sahojas, ajirāprabhu, vaiśvānara, naryāpas, paṅktirādhas and visarpin. Some YV traditions have the directional devatas instead of these forms.

4. uttarāṅgamantra to saptavant agni"

(muṇḍaka 2.1.8) preserves a mantra to saptavant typically used in uttarāṅga of agnimukham. We use a different shaunaka version. sapta prāṇāḥ prabhavanti tasmātsaptārciṣaḥ samidhaḥ sapta homāḥ| sapta ime lokā yeṣu caranti prāṇā guhāśayā nihitāḥ sapta sapta || (agnaye saptavate svāhā)

brahmavidyā recipient eligibility and atharvan connection:

(muṇḍaka 3.2.11) kriyāvantaḥ śrotriyā brahmāniṣṭhāḥ svayaṃ juhvata ekarṣiṃ śraddhayantaḥ | teṣām evaitāṃ brahmāvidyāṃ vadeta śirovrataṃ vidhivad yais tu cīrṇam || The terminal section of the upaniṣad enumerates the qualities of a recipient who is eligible to receive the brahmavidyā taught in this upaniṣad.

“Engaged in the action/practice, well versed in in vedas, grounded in brahman, who themselves sacrifice in ekarṣi, full of faith and who have completed the shirovrata as per specifications”.

Two specific eligibility criteria puts this text squarely in the atharvan camp.

(a) Offering themselves in ekarṣi and (b) śirovratam.

I haven’t seen a single commentator who got these two right. This is mainly due to poor knowledge of atharvan karams outside AV circles. The issue is compounded by loss of tradition even within AV circles.

Let us examine these twO:

1. śirovratam

Now, just like various veda vratas a brahmachāri student observes in other vedas, AV student observes his set of prescribed vratas: Atharva pariśiṣṭa 49.4.11 deals with this topic and names 18 vratas: (AVParis_49,4.11) tatra brahmavede ‘ṣṭādaśa vratāni cariṣyan sāvitrīvratam | vedavratam | vedottaravratam | mailavratam | mailottaravratam | mṛgāravratam | rohitavratam | viṣāsahivratam | yamavratam | śāntivratam | śikhivratam | gaṇavratam | śirovratam | śikhāvratam | marudvratam | adhivratam | aṅgirovratam | pāśupatavrataṃ caret || Perceptive reader may observe that various vratas map to specific sections of atharvan shruti. I performed the sāvtrīvratam. viśāsahi vratam is an important one in AV circles and the viśāsahi chapter is there in both shaunaka and paippalāda. The ritual is performed to indrāviṣṇū on solar and lunar eclipse days or on a fullmoon around September. A circular cake disk is made out of wheat or yava flour and liberally coated with ghee and jaggery. Balls of nīvāra rice mixed with ghee is also offered. The performer brings the ritual fire on his head in a pot and brings it to the sacrifical spot chanting the AprI sūkta. Most likely, this element of carrying the ritual fire on head was the reason some commentators mistook the viśāsahi vratam for the śirovratam. AV circles as well as the pariśiṣṭa reckons śirovratam as the vrata one observes to study a text called atharvashiras. Now, the identity of the atharvashiras is lost. Modern practice is to consider AV-S tṛca 20.41 “indro dadhīco asthabhiḥ” as atharvashiras (second mantra has “icchannaśvasya yacchiraḥ” - the allusion to dadhīci the atharvan and the horse head is alluded to in RV as well as jaiminīyabrahmaṇa). It is very unlikely that this is the original AV’s atharvashiras, despite YV tradition calling a longer sūkta by this name. I’ve heard some take this vrata for atharvashiras Upanishad. I propose the candidate paippalāda 16.59, taking the liṅga from 10th mantra: tad vā atharvaṇaḥ śiro devakośaḥ samubjitaḥ | tat prāṇo abhi rakṣatu śriyam annam atho manaḥ || Nevertheless, the śirovratam is of atharvanic provenance.

2. “svayaṃ juhvata ekarṣiṃ”

What is this ekarṣi fire that the recipient personally sacrifices? Commentators are unlikely to tell you.

You need to resort to paippalāda 9.21.1, full passage. To set the context, AV practice has a plethora of savayajñas cooked in brahmaudana fire. In modern time, someone who wishes to setup śrauta fires undergo short consecration period. His gṛhya fire (50% or 100%) are taken to setup the brahmaudana fire, food for the priests are cooked, distributed, yajamAna and family stays awake at night, playing dice games etc, and the agnyAdheya rite starts later. Consecration period is ideally one year and AV tradition has prescription for various sava yajñas during this period. Yajamana and wife practice various prescribed observances. They only eat sacrificial food (haviṣyabhakṣā syur – kaushika gṛhya sUtra)

AVP 9.2.1: yo vā ekaśarāvaṃ nirvaped ekarṣim evānu nirvapet | Whoever should offer a single bowl of oblation, he should offer it following after the Single Seer, ekarṣi. The single śarāva/shallow bowl nirvapaṇa (pouring out) is actually done with the mantra “devasya tvā savituḥ prasave aśvinorbāhubhyāṃ pūṣṇo hastābhyām ekarṣaye tvā juṣṭam nirvapāmi”. eṣa vā ekarṣir yad agniḥ | Agni, indeed, is the Single Seer, ekarṣi. ekarṣiṃ caiva lokaṃ cāvarundhe | He wins both the Single Seer and the world. ekarṣir iva tapaty ekarṣir iva dīdāyaikarṣir ivānnādo bhavati ya evaṃ veda | He who knows this shines like the Single Seer, glows like the Single Seer, and becomes an eater of food like the Single Seer. sa ya evaṃ vidvān prāśnīyād etām eva devatāṃ manasā dhyāyed| He who, knowing this, should consume [the remnant/havis shesha], should meditate mentally upon this very deity, saying: ekarṣes tvā cakṣuṣā paśyāmy| “I behold you with the eye of the Single Seer” ekarṣes tvā hastābhyām ā rabha| “I take hold of you with the hands of the Single Seer.” ekarṣes tvāsyena prāśnāmy| “I consume you with the mouth of the Single Seer” ekarṣes tvā jaṭhare sādayāmīti | “I place you within the belly of the Single Seer.” sa yathā hutam iṣṭaṃ prāśnīyād evainaṃ prāśnāti || Just as one might consume what has been offered and sacrificed, even so does he consume it.

The actual yajña has recitation of various mantras and sūktas, but this for ekaśarāva/ekarṣi ritual, this is sufficient. You can compare this with another sAvitrI rite from kaushika sUtra where paste of fruits and few other things are ritually consumed that I tweeted in the past. Note the sambandhas established - the external fire is mapped to internal fire and consumption of the havis by the agni is ritually mapped to the ritual consumption of the cooked food by the yajña participants.

brahmaloka

IMO the muNDaka redactor is caught napping with this. The oblations in a perfectly performed agnihotra already leads the yajamāna to brahmaloka. Missing this forces later commentators to either invent a loka of a lower brahman or interpret this as svarga.

Conclusion

To conclude, muṇḍaka text has an atharvanic core that will make sense only in AV ritual setting which got an extreme aupaniṣada reworking.

Despite the reworking and a delivery of gut punch to karman, it still didn’t manage to sever the AV ties to atharvanic rituals.
